FILE: Data centers.docx
======================================================================

Data centers
Data centers are becoming one of the defining infrastructure and economic issues facing Washington County and Oregon.
I support responsible data center development because technology, artificial intelligence, cloud computing, and digital infrastructure are increasingly critical to the modern economy. These facilities can strengthen Oregon’s long-term economic competitiveness and support high-value industries.
However, growth must occur responsibly and sustainably.
Residents are right to be concerned about rising electricity demand, water consumption, land use pressures, utility costs, and the impact on local infrastructure and tax fairness. Oregon families should not bear higher costs so multinational corporations can receive unlimited subsidies without accountability.
I do not support reckless expansion that strains our electrical grid, threatens groundwater resources, or places disproportionate burdens on local communities.
I believe future data center development should meet clear standards regarding:power generation,water sustainability,environmental stewardship,infrastructure investment,and fair tax contribution.
In particular, I strongly support expanding modern nuclear energy — especially Small Modular Reactor (SMR) technology — as part of America’s long-term energy strategy.
Modern nuclear technology is dramatically safer, cleaner, more reliable, and more energy-dense than many people realize. Unlike intermittent energy sources, nuclear power provides stable baseload electricity capable of supporting growing industrial and technological demand without dramatically increasing carbon emissions.
If Oregon intends to support advanced manufacturing, semiconductor growth, artificial intelligence infrastructure, and data centers, then we must also be honest about the enormous energy requirements involved.
We cannot continue pursuing electrification policies while simultaneously restricting reliable energy generation.
I support an ‘all-of-the-above’ energy strategy that includes hydroelectric, natural gas, renewables, and next-generation nuclear power to ensure Oregon families and businesses have access to affordable, reliable energy for decades to come.
Rather than imposing blanket moratoriums driven by ideology, Oregon should pursue smart regulation that protects communities while encouraging responsible innovation, infrastructure investment, and long-term energy independence.
We need balanced leadership that understands both economic growth and resource stewardship — not reactionary policies that simply drive investment and jobs out of Oregon.”

FILE: Education.docx
======================================================================

Education
Oregon’s children deserve far better than what they are receiving today. Despite spending billions of taxpayer dollars on education, our state now ranks near the bottom nationally in core academic performance, including reading and math. That should concern every parent, taxpayer, employer, and community member regardless of political party.
This is not a criticism of our teachers. Oregon has many dedicated, hardworking educators who care deeply about their students. The problem is that for too long, state education leadership and policymakers have lost focus on academic fundamentals.
While other states aggressively implemented evidence-based reading and math reforms, strengthened accountability, expanded instructional support, and focused intensely on student achievement, Oregon moved too slowly and often prioritized bureaucracy, ideology, and equity frameworks over measurable academic success.
Families are frustrated because they see the results:declining reading proficiency,poor math performance,classroom disruption,chronic absenteeism,and students graduating without the skills they need for success in college, trades, military service, or the workforce.
Oregon schools should first and foremost focus on reading, writing, mathematics, science, civics, discipline, and critical thinking.
I support legislation that returns Oregon education to the basics:evidence-based reading instruction grounded in the science of reading,strong math fundamentals,clear academic standards,classroom accountability,parent transparency,and safe, orderly learning environments.
At the same time, I believe local school districts need greater flexibility to meet the unique needs of their own students and communities rather than being forced into one-size-fits-all mandates from Salem.
Our teachers should be empowered to teach.Parents should be respected as partners.And students should leave school truly prepared for adult life.
We can care about every child while still insisting on excellence, accountability, and measurable academic achievement. In fact, that is exactly what Oregon families expect from their education system.”

FILE: Public safety.docx
======================================================================

Public safety is one of the most basic responsibilities of government. Families should feel safe in their homes, children should feel safe walking to school, seniors should feel safe using public transportation, and businesses should feel confident investing in their communities.
Unfortunately, many Oregonians no longer feel that basic sense of security.
Over the past several years, Oregon has experienced rising public frustration over crime, drug activity, repeat offenders, property crime, public disorder, and slow emergency response times — while many law enforcement agencies continue struggling with staffing shortages, low morale, retirements, and difficulty recruiting new officers.
Oregon already ranks near the bottom nationally in law enforcement officers per capita. At a time when communities are asking for greater visibility, faster response times, and stronger community engagement, many departments are operating under increasing strain.
I support fully funding public safety and ensuring that law enforcement agencies have the personnel, training, technology, and resources necessary to protect our communities effectively and professionally.
This includes:improving recruitment and retention,supporting competitive compensation,expanding behavioral health and crisis response partnerships,strengthening community policing,improving coordination between local agencies,and ensuring officers receive high-quality training in de-escalation, mental health response, and constitutional policing.
Supporting law enforcement and supporting accountability are not mutually exclusive.
Most officers serve honorably and professionally under increasingly difficult conditions. They deserve leadership that supports both effective policing and public trust.
I also believe Oregon must address the broader policy failures contributing to public disorder, including untreated addiction, mental illness, chronic homelessness, weak prosecution of repeat offenders, and policies that too often cycle individuals endlessly through emergency systems without meaningful intervention or accountability.
Public safety cannot exist without functioning mental health systems, addiction treatment, and coordinated community support services.
At the same time, communities cannot thrive when laws are inconsistently enforced or when criminal behavior is normalized in the name of ideology.
I support practical, balanced public safety policies that combine:strong law enforcement,community partnership,mental health resources,treatment access,accountability,and safe neighborhoods for everyone.
Oregonians should not have to choose between compassion and safety.
We must have both

FILE: Small businesses are the backbone of Oregon.docx
======================================================================

Small businesses are the backbone of Oregon’s economy, yet for years they have been increasingly burdened by the tax-and-spend mentality coming out of Salem.
Family-owned businesses, independent contractors, restaurants, farms, retailers, service providers, medical practices, and startups are struggling under growing layers of taxes, regulations, permit delays, labor mandates, rising utility costs, insurance costs, and bureaucratic complexity.
Many business owners no longer feel that state government sees them as partners in economic growth. Instead, they often feel treated as an endless source of revenue to fund expanding government programs and political priorities.
Oregon has become increasingly difficult and expensive to operate a business in.
Rising payroll taxes,the Corporate Activity Tax,escalating workers compensation and insurance costs,increasing energy prices driven by policies such as the Climate Protection Program,complex labor regulations,and permitting delays have all combined to create an environment where many entrepreneurs are questioning whether they can continue to grow — or even survive — in Oregon.
Small businesses cannot simply absorb endless new costs. Those costs eventually result in:higher prices for consumers,reduced hiring,reduced wages,fewer local investments,or businesses closing entirely.
Meanwhile, large corporations often have the resources, attorneys, accountants, and political influence necessary to navigate these systems far more easily than small local employers.
That is backwards.
Oregon should be creating an environment where small businesses can thrive, innovate, hire employees, and strengthen local communities.
One of my first legislative priorities will be introducing and supporting policies specifically focused on small business growth, regulatory reform, and economic opportunity.
I support:reducing unnecessary regulations and duplicative permitting requirements,streamlining licensing and approval processes,reviewing and limiting excessive fee increases,improving access to startup capital and workforce training,protecting independent contractors and small employers from overly burdensome mandates,and requiring stronger economic impact review before major new business regulations are adopted.
I also support targeted tax relief for small businesses and reforms that encourage reinvestment, expansion, and job creation within Oregon communities.
State government should actively evaluate whether regulations and taxes disproportionately harm small businesses compared to large corporations.
We should be making it easier — not harder — for hardworking Oregonians to start businesses, create jobs, and build successful futures for their families.
Oregon’s economy will not be strengthened by expanding bureaucracy and dependency.
It will be strengthened by supporting entrepreneurship, rewarding hard work, encouraging innovation, and restoring an economic environment where small businesses can once again succeed.”

FILE: Transportation and energy.docx
======================================================================

Transportation and energy
Oregonians deserve transportation and energy policies grounded in reality, affordability, and measurable results — not ideology.
The transportation landscape has fundamentally changed since 2020.
Office vacancy rates remain high.Remote and hybrid work have permanently reduced daily commuter traffic into downtown Portland.And TriMet ridership remains dramatically below its historical peak — roughly one-third lower than its highest ridership levels from the early 2010s.
Yet despite these major shifts, some political leaders continue pushing enormously expensive rail expansion projects and transportation policies based on outdated assumptions about commuter behavior and urban growth.
I support public transportation. But public transportation must serve actual rider behavior and real community needs — not ideological agendas or unrealistic planning models.
Today, buses already carry a larger share of riders in many corridors because they are:more flexible,less expensive,faster to deploy,easier to reroute,and better able to adapt as population and employment patterns evolve.
Before Oregon commits taxpayers to additional multi-billion-dollar rail projects, we must honestly evaluate:long-term ridership trends,operating deficits,public safety concerns,financial sustainability,and whether these systems truly serve the majority of Oregon families and workers.
This conversation also cannot be separated from Oregon’s broader energy and climate policies.
Under former Governor Kate Brown, Oregon implemented the Climate Protection Program — a sweeping regulatory system that is already increasing energy and utility costs for Oregonians while having virtually no measurable impact on global climate outcomes.
Oregonians are now seeing these costs directly on their utility bills through new climate-related charges and rate increases. These policies affect not only gasoline and natural gas prices, but also the cost of food, shipping, manufacturing, housing, and nearly every aspect of daily life.
Oregon families are being asked to pay significantly more for energy and transportation at a time when many are already struggling with inflation, housing costs, and affordability.
Meanwhile, Oregon’s contribution to global carbon emissions is extremely small on a worldwide scale. Even drastic reductions within Oregon would have little measurable effect on global temperatures unless major industrial nations around the world make similar changes.
That does not mean environmental stewardship is unimportant. It absolutely matters.
But good policy requires balancing environmental goals with economic reality, affordability, reliability, and scientific honesty about actual impact.
I support an ‘all-of-the-above’ energy strategy that includes hydroelectric, natural gas, renewables, and next-generation nuclear power — especially Small Modular Reactor technology — to provide stable, affordable, low-emission energy for the future.
We cannot continue pursuing aggressive electrification policies while simultaneously restricting reliable power generation and driving up costs for working families.
Oregon needs practical transportation and energy policies focused on:affordability,mobility,reliability,public safety,and measurable public benefit.
We should invest in infrastructure that people actually use, energy systems that reliably work, and policies that strengthen Oregon families rather than burdening them with ever-rising costs and ideological experiments.”

FILE: Universal health care.docx
======================================================================

As a veterinarian and medical professional who has spent nearly 30 years caring for patients and families, I understand many of the frustrations people feel with the healthcare system. Costs are too high. Insurance bureaucracy is overwhelming. Healthcare workers are increasingly burned out. And too many families delay care because they are worried about affordability.
But Oregon families deserve solutions grounded in reality — not ideological promises that risk making healthcare even more expensive, less accessible, and more controlled by government bureaucracy.
I do not support a government-run single-payer healthcare system for Oregon.
Oregonians have already seen what happens when large systems become over-centralized, poorly managed, and disconnected from the people they serve. We should be very cautious about placing the entire healthcare system under expanded state control when Oregon is already struggling to effectively manage core responsibilities such as education, public safety, transportation, and behavioral health services.
A state-run universal healthcare system would almost certainly require massive new taxes on working families, employers, and healthcare providers while creating additional bureaucracy, workforce shortages, and longer wait times for care. It also risks driving physicians, specialists, and healthcare businesses out of Oregon at a time when access to care is already strained in many communities.
I believe healthcare reform should focus on practical improvements that increase affordability, access, transparency, and patient choice — not turning healthcare decisions over to politicians and state agencies.
My priorities include:
- increasing access to primary and preventive care,
- reducing unnecessary administrative burdens on providers,
- improving price transparency,
- encouraging competition and innovation,
- supporting independent medical practices,
- strengthening rural and community healthcare access,
- expanding workforce training,
- and addressing mental health and addiction treatment in practical, measurable ways.
I also believe healthcare decisions should remain primarily between patients and their healthcare professionals — not centralized through expanding layers of government control.
Compassion matters deeply. But compassion must also be paired with competence, sustainability, accountability, and honesty about costs and consequences.
Oregon families are already struggling with rising housing costs, energy bills, taxes, and daily expenses. The answer is not another massive government system funded by even higher taxation and broader bureaucracy.
We need healthcare reform that is practical, financially responsible, patient-centered, and focused on delivering real results for Oregonians.”

Veteran support
As a retired Navy veteran, I understand that military service leaves a lasting impact long after a uniform is removed. Veterans carry with them experiences, sacrifices, responsibilities, and challenges that many civilians may never fully understand.
While military service builds discipline, leadership, resilience, and purpose, many veterans also face very real struggles transitioning back into civilian life. Too many veterans continue to struggle with homelessness, unemployment or underemployment, barriers to medical care, mental health challenges, substance abuse, social isolation, and navigating increasingly complex government systems.
It is my intention to identify, address, and help resolve the issues affecting our veteran community to the very best of my abilities.
Our veterans fulfilled their commitment to this nation. Government and society should fulfill their commitment to them.
I support improving access to:quality healthcare,mental health services,addiction treatment,housing assistance,workforce training,career transition support,and employment opportunities for veterans and military families.
I also believe Oregon should strengthen partnerships between veterans organizations, healthcare providers, local communities, employers, and state agencies to ensure veterans do not fall through bureaucratic cracks while trying to obtain the services they earned through their service.
Veterans possess tremendous leadership, technical skill, work ethic, and experience that continue benefiting our communities long after military retirement.
My oath did not end when I took off the uniform.
Service is not simply a career. It is a lifelong responsibility.
I will continue working to support veterans, defend the values we served to protect, and ensure those who sacrificed for this country are treated with the dignity, respect, and support they deserve.”
