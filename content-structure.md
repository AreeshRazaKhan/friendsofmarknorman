# PROJECT BRIEF

## Mark G. Norman Campaign Website — Content Generation

### Project Background

We are running an ongoing political campaign project under our company, Operation 1776 (op1776.com). Through this initiative, we design, build, and deploy professional campaign websites for candidates competing in elections across various U.S. states. Each website is custom-built around the candidate's voice, background, policy positions, and brand identity, while meeting modern web standards and campaign-finance compliance requirements.

### This Assignment

This brief covers the campaign website for Mark G. Norman, who is running for the Oregon House of Representatives, District 27, as a Republican in the 2026 election cycle. The task is to generate complete, polished, ready-to-publish copy for every section, every field, and every page defined in the website's content structure below.

### Reference Materials

Two documents are being provided for this work:

1. Content Structure (included in full below) — the single source of truth for the website. It lists every page in render order, every section, every individual content field with current placeholder copy, current word counts, and recommended word caps, plus 4–8 photography keywords for each image slot. It tells you exactly what content is needed, where it goes, and how long it can be.
2. Onboarding Form (provided separately) — a document completed by Mark Norman himself, containing his personal background, biographical details, descriptions, talking points, policy preferences, and the overall vision he has for the tone and direction of his campaign messaging. It tells you who the candidate is and how he wants to sound.

### Instructions

Please plug both files — the Content Structure and the Onboarding Form — into your AI tool of choice and use them together to draft the final campaign content. The Content Structure tells you what to write and how long it should be; the Onboarding Form tells you what the candidate actually wants to say and the spirit in which he wants to say it. The two files are complementary, not redundant, and the best output will draw from both.

### Quality Bar

The output must:

- Be accurate and faithful to Mark's voice, story, and stated positions as expressed in the Onboarding Form.
- Strictly respect every word cap defined in the Content Structure — these are not suggestions; they protect the visual rhythm of the brand.
- Follow the brand's editorial tone (Service • Solutions • Accountability) — practical, grounded, plainspoken; never slogan-y or rehearsed.
- Avoid generic political clichés, empty rhetoric, and AI-flavored padding.
- Pass legal and compliance review, particularly for FAQs, donation copy, A2P SMS disclosures, and the Privacy / Terms of Service sections.
- Match the photography keywords defined for each image slot — authentic, naturally lit, eye-level, candid; never stock, never overproduced.

In short: the content should sound like Mark — a Navy veteran, veterinarian, and small-business owner from Washington County who is running because he genuinely believes Oregon families deserve practical leadership — and it should slot cleanly into the structure outlined below without any modification to the structure itself.

The complete, unmodified Content Structure  document follows.

## How to extend this document

When adding a new page or section:
1. Copy the section template (eyebrow / H2 / lead / fields / image keywords).
2. Set a word cap that matches the visual rhythm of the brand.
3. If a new image slot is added, pick keywords from the library above; never use stock.
4. Re-export this document to PDF after material changes so legal/compliance has a current snapshot.
