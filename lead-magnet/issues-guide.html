Understanding the Choice
03

What socialism means in public policy.

At its core, socialism is a political and economic philosophy that seeks a much larger role for government in providing services, directing economic activity, redistributing wealth, and controlling major parts of the economy through taxation, regulation, public programs, and public ownership.

In practical terms, that often means more decisions being shifted away from families, entrepreneurs, local communities, private employers, and individual citizens, and toward government agencies, public systems, and centralized planning.

Mark believes that approach is wrong for Oregon.

He believes government has important responsibilities, but it should not become the center of economic life, family decision-making, healthcare decisions, small-business survival, energy policy, transportation planning, or local community direction.

Understanding the Choice
04

What the Democratic Socialists of America supports.

The Democratic Socialists of America, or DSA, describes itself as the largest socialist organization in the United States and says it believes working people should run both the economy and society democratically.

DSA’s 2024 program includes support for higher taxes on wealthy earners and corporations, Medicare for All, expanded labor power, and public ownership over major transportation and energy infrastructure and natural resources.

Mark believes voters should understand what that means.

This is not simply a normal debate about spending more on schools, healthcare, or housing. It is a much broader debate about who should control major parts of the economy and public life.

Mark believes those decisions should remain rooted in individual freedom, private enterprise, local communities, accountable government, and constitutional limits — not a socialist model of expanded government control.

The Role of Government
05

Government should serve people, not simply grow larger.

Mark believes Oregon needs reform, accountability, and compassion.

But he does not believe Oregon needs to replace capitalism, weaken private enterprise, or move major sectors of the economy under public ownership.

Large government-centered systems can create serious unintended consequences:

  • Higher taxes and fees
  • Higher energy and utility costs
  • More regulation
  • More bureaucracy
  • Less local flexibility
  • Fewer choices
  • Slower services
  • Programs measured by spending instead of results
Mark believes America’s success has been built on individual liberty, private property rights, entrepreneurship, free enterprise, strong families, local communities, and the opportunity for people to improve their lives through hard work and personal initiative.

Why This Matters

Why this matters in House District 27.

This issue is not theoretical. House District 27 voters deserve to understand the different governing philosophies being presented to them. This election is about more than personalities. One view depends on larger government, higher taxes, more centralized control, and expanded public systems.

  • He supports helping vulnerable Oregonians, but he believes public programs must be accountable, effective, and measured by results.
