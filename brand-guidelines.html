N° 01 — Foundation
BRAND FOUNDATION

Design Principles.

Six principles ground every decision — from a yard sign on a country road to a hero section on the website. They reflect the candidate himself: a Navy veteran, longtime veterinarian, and small-business owner running on practical leadership for House District 27.

PRINCIPLE 01

Practical, not partisan.

Lead with solutions, not slogans. Frame every issue around the family at the kitchen table, not the party platform.

PRINCIPLE 02

Calm & steady.

Speak plainly. Avoid outrage. Oregon needs adults in the room — that posture shapes typography, color, and tone.

PRINCIPLE 03

Experienced.

30 years of service — Navy, vet, small business — without lecturing.

PRINCIPLE 04

Accountable.

Spend taxpayer dollars carefully. Measure results, not effort.

PRINCIPLE 05

Respectful.

Disagree without being disagreeable. Every voter, every time.

PRINCIPLE 06

Honest, brief, and earned.

Assume the reader is busy, smart, and skeptical. Be brief. Be honest. Earn the next sentence — that rule applies to copy, photography, and layout density alike.

N° 08 — WCAG
ACCESSIBILITY

Designed to be read.

Every approved color pair clears WCAG 2.2 AA at body size or better. Type sizes respect older voters. Focus states never disappear. The brand should be just as legible in a kitchen at dawn as on a phone in bright Oregon sun.

Body text 16px+. Minimum on web. Print copy never below 12pt.
Focus visible. 2px red outline + 2px paper offset on every interactive element.
Color is never the only signal. Pair red with weight, label, or icon.
Tap targets ≥ 44 × 44px. Buttons and form fields meet WCAG 2.5.5.
Alt text mandatory. Decorative images get alt=""; informative ones get a sentence.
No critical info in red alone. Use Navy for primary, Red for emphasis only.
Captioned video. Every campaign video ships with English captions and a transcript.
Form labels never hidden. Placeholder ≠ label. Always provide both.

N° 09 — Direction
PHOTOGRAPHY & IMAGERY

Authentic moments.

Photography is human-centered, naturally lit, and never overproduced. Mark in the room with constituents. The dogs (Doug the sheepadoodle leads the pack) when natural. No suit-and-podium clichés. No heavy filters. The voter should recognize the moment from real life.

Headshot · primaryEye-line at camera
ConstituentsTown hall
At the practiceVeterinary work
Lifestyle · candidate-in-communityOutdoor light · with the dogs
Small business visitListening posture
Service · NavyArchive only when relevant
Family · candidUse sparingly

Do

  • Shoot in natural daylight whenever possible.
  • Eye-level. Candid. Listening, not posing.
  • True skin tones — no orange grading.
  • Show the hands of working Oregonians.
  • Crop tight on the subject. Clean backgrounds.

Don't

  • Stiff suit-and-podium photography.
  • Heavy color grading or fake skin tones.
  • Generic stock photography. Ever.
  • Background clutter, busy crowds, mid-blink.
  • Flag-draped backgrounds — restraint over rah-rah.

N° 10 — Language
DESIGN PATTERNS

A pattern language.

Repeatable motifs that turn a yard sign, a mailer, and an Instagram story into the same campaign. Use sparingly — one motif per layout — and keep them in service of the message, never as decoration for its own sake.

01 · Civic Stripe Field

Stripes & halftone.

Navy ground with red diagonal pinstripes at 45°, layered with a subtle paper-tone halftone. Civic without the flag-waving. Use behind hero quotes, on door hangers, and as a full-bleed event backdrop.

stripe 2px · 24px gap · halftone 14px · 45°
02 · Tilted Stamp

Rotated badge.

For tagline drops and one-line CTAs. Hard offset shadow, no soft blur.

Values, set in oversize Cabin.

The campaign values rendered as the headline itself — no decorative noise. Used at the close of long-form pages and in the footer of mailers.

PRACTICAL. Accountable. STEADY.
Pattern Rules
One motif per layout. All patterns share the same vocabulary: 45° angles, 12–24px rhythm, navy & red only. Patterns sit behind copy, never on top of it. Decorative density caps at 40% area. Never combine stripes & halftone & stars in the same frame.

Never do

  • Recolor, stretch, rotate, or container the logo.
  • Use Red as a background for body copy.
  • Put critical info in Red alone.
  • Use stock photography or staged podium shots.
  • Mix any other typeface with Cabin.
  • Drop body type below 12pt in print.

Always do

  • Lead with one primary action per screen.
  • Use Navy 60% / White 30% / Red 8% / Black 2%.
  • Pair Red with weight, label, or icon.
  • Hit WCAG 2.2 AA on every color pair.
  • Rotate badges 5–8° for energy, never copy.
  • Sign every print piece with the PAC disclosure.
